## Jason's one change
"Not running at altitude." First 50 OK; after that, breathing couldn't keep up and climbing got
progressively harder. Max achievable HR on climbs fell across the race: **~145 → 135 → 120 → ~117
at the end while climbing as hard as he felt he could.**
**Claude's note:** altitude certainly contributes (hypoxia lowers max HR), but a falling HR ceiling
is also the classic signature of accumulated fatigue and low glycogen — and the final climb was
under-fuelled (below). Don't attribute it to altitude alone until fuel is fixed. Open question:
does "not at altitude" mean skip altitude races, or acclimatise differently?

## HYPOTHESIS — the Monster cans and both bloating episodes
Night caffeine was **12-oz pink Monster cans**: one carried out of MM2, one at HC3, one at HC4
(the written plan was G Fuel in HOT water — non-carbonated). Timeline:
- Monster #1 out of MM2 → **very bloated on the way to HC3** → crisis after HC3
- Monster #2 at HC3 (11:42 PM)
- Monster #3 at HC4 (5:24 AM) → **bloated again ~7-8 AM**
Both bloating episodes followed a can. Plausible mechanisms: carbonation (gas expands ~40-50% at
altitude); ~3 cans ≈ 330-360 mg caffeine over ~7 hrs on a system caffeine-free for two weeks
(comfort zone was 100-150 mg); caffeine as a gastric irritant; and — if it was a zero-sugar
Monster Ultra — erythritol, a sugar alcohol that commonly causes gas and bloating. **Not proven.
Test next season; mention to the GI doctor.** Fix to try: non-carbonated caffeine (G Fuel hot,
per the original plan, or tabs), one dose at a time.

## LESSON — "bloated and unsure" → he cut food, water AND sodium
On the way to HC3, bloated and unsure whether it was too much or too little, he **cut all three**.
He didn't realise how deep the hole was until leaving HC3. Cutting everything turned an
uncomfortable stomach into a calorie and sodium deficit — the same loop he identified after
Six-0 (under-fuelled + under-sodium → slower emptying → can't eat). It's also the exact question
he asked mid-race the next morning. **New decision rule:** when bloated and unsure, don't cut
everything. Keep sodium steady, stop carbonation, and take a **timed 15-20 min full stop**
(asleep if possible) — the nap is the one intervention that demonstrably worked.

## The nap — his call, against the written plan
The plan said "Do not sit." At Venable 2 he decided that stillness would restore gut blood flow
better than anything else, napped 20 minutes in a volunteer's car, and turned it around. Good
judgement overriding a rule. Rule revised in fueling-notes.

## CLAUDE ERROR — the MM3 ziplock under-fuelled the final Music Pass spur
MM3 ziplock was 3 Huma + 1 PF90 (~660 cal), sized as if the spur took ~3 hrs, like it did at
hour 1. At hour 33 it took far longer. He ate everything on the climb and had **nothing for the
descent** — so he came down cautiously rather than fast. **Next time: size the final spur for
~4-5 hrs, at least double the gels, and save some for the descent.** (Opening climb at hour 1
used 2 Huma up + 1 down.)
